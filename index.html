Florea is a citizen science platform where the public and taxonomists work together to identify Endangered, Rare and Threatened plants and build an open plant encyclopedia.

How Florea works

Three ways to support plant data collection

Explorer

Browse the encyclopedia

Search plant records by family, genus, and species, including origin location and classification.

Identify from a photo

Contribute

Explorer

Start with four plant families

Dipterocarpaceae

Includes genera such as Vatica, Dipterocarpus, and Hopea. Many species are threatened rainforest trees.

Lauraceae

Includes Actinodaphne, Endiandra, and Beilschmiedia, often found in tropical forest habitats.

Burseraceae

Includes Canarium, Dacryodes, and Santiria. Useful records for mapping remaining forest stands.

Myristicaceae

Includes Horsfieldia, Knema, and Myristica. These nutmeg-family trees are used in biodiversity surveys.

Why this project exists

Malaysia is a biodiversity hotspot under pressure.

Population growth and agriculture place increasing pressure on natural ecosystems. Citizen science can increase both the amount of plant data collected and the number of people helping scientists monitor Endangered, Rare and Threatened species.

  • Explore plant families, genera, and species in one encyclopedia.
  • Identify plants from photos, including scientific and common names.
  • Contribute observations after you register and log in.
  • Send an enquiry if you need help from taxonomists or the project team.
